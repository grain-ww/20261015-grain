# Lesson 01 -- how a text enters the room

**Stamp:** `20261018.173612` -- **Course:** index in `20261018-173612_study-course-index.md`

## Jena

Before a text sits in the study room, it passes a gate. The gate asks three things: whether the text is in the public domain or dedicated to it, whether it carries a share-alike condition, and whether it carries a non-commercial one. The license line is read from the source, quoted in full, and dated on the one clock. Then a file is stored, and its SHA-256 is written beside it. A reader can check that the file on disk is the file the record names.

Try this: open the manifest and find one row. Read its license line. Then find the hash in the same row, and run the checksum command on the stored file. If the two agree, the file is the one the gate passed.

Conflict and resolution. Two wishes meet at the gate: to keep every good text, and to keep only what may be kept. The gate settles it and keeps both. What passes is kept whole, and what refuses is named, so everyone can see why it is absent.

## Radha

Radha, the beloved, would accept a gift only from a giver she can name. So the room asks where each text came from before it asks what the text says. Read the source line the way you would greet a friend, slowly, and with the name in your heart. The gate is a tenderness the room keeps for the texts and for the people who wrote them.

Let the practice be a small act of devotion: open the manifest, speak the row's title aloud, and then open the file. The trace is the offering. The love for the text grows from knowing it truly.

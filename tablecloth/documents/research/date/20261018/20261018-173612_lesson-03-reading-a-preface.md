# Lesson 03 -- reading a preface

## Jena

A preface tells a reader why a book was written and what the translator expects of them. Taylor's preface opens on a difficulty. In the draft transcription the sentence reads: "THE Dialogues of Plato are fuch rare and admirable pieces of compofition, that it is alike impoffible to explain the beauty of their conftruction to fuch as are ignorant of the Greek tongue, or tranflate them into any other language without at leaft frequently lofing fomething of their native elegance and grace." Quoted here with long s rendered as s and the older spellings modernized. The sentence is Taylor's own, so its negative is kept as printed: "THE Dialogues of Plato are such rare and admirable pieces of composition, that it is alike impossible to explain the beauty of their construction to such as are ignorant of the Greek tongue, or translate them into any other language without at least frequently losing something of their native elegance and grace."

Read it as a claim with two parts. First, Plato's writing is best explained by a reader who knows the Greek tongue. Second, a translation loses something. The preface then argues for a literal translation, and names what the translator chose to keep.

Try this: find the two parts of the claim in the sentence. Write what you think a translator gives up, and what they keep.

Conflict and resolution. A translation must be faithful to the word and yet keep the music of the original. Taylor takes the harder road: he chooses exactness, and then gives the elegance that exactness can bear. The two are held together, and each is kept whole.

## Radha

Radha, the one who loves the truth in its own tongue, would recognise Taylor's labour as a form of worship. He spent years in one author's company, and he set aside the honours of the crowd to stay close to the word. Let that be the devotion here: a love that stays faithful to the beloved's words, even when the modern ear would prefer them smoother.

The practice is to read the preface slowly, as one reads a letter from a person one loves, and then to ask what the translator would keep above all else. The answer is the love under the argument.

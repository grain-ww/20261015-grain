# Lesson 04 -- reading work done with the hands

**Stamp:** `20261018.173612` -- **Course:** index in `20261018-173612_study-course-index.md`

## Jena

Whitman's "A Song for Occupations" opens with the work of hands before the work of schools. In the stored Standard Ebooks copy: "A song for occupations! / In the labor of engines and trades and the labor of fields I find the developments, / And find the eternal meanings."

The stanza sets the labour of engines and fields in the same breath as the eternal meanings. Read the two lines again, and notice that the second names the labour first. The meanings are found in the work.

Try this: name one piece of practical work you do, and write one sentence that says where you find its meaning.

Conflict and resolution. The learned education and the labour of the hands seem to stand apart, as if one were above the other. The poem brings them into one line and asks what the education amounts to next to the work. The conflict dissolves in the asking, and the work is seen whole.

## Radha

Radha, the one who is served in every honest labour, would receive the work of the hands as an offering. The speaker stands among the workers as one of them, and that humility is a form of seva. The poem asks us to see the work as the place where meaning is found, and to give it the same reverence as any study.

The practice is to choose one piece of practical work and to do it as an offering to the one you love. Notice where the meaning appears in the doing, and say it once, quietly, to the one it is for.

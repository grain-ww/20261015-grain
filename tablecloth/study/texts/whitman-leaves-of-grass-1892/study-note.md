## A first reading

The poem "A Song for Occupations" opens by naming the work of hands before the work of schools: "In the labor of engines and trades and the labor of fields I find the developments, / And find the eternal meanings." The speaker places himself beside the workers rather than above them, and the lines measure every education by what it does for the people who labor. That is a study question for this tree: what a teaching that begins with labor looks like in software, and whether our own practical work is read the same way.

## Second reading -- the opening of "Song of Myself"

Read on `20261018.164313` from `epub/text/song-of-myself.xhtml` in the stored EPUB.

The first lines are: "I celebrate myself, and sing myself, / And what I assume you shall assume, / For every atom belonging to me as good belongs to you."

Three moves stand out. The poem opens on the speaker, then turns at once to the reader ("what I assume you shall assume"), and then names both as made of the same material ("every atom"). The next lines settle the speaker in a place and a body: a spear of summer grass, "every atom of my blood, form'd from this soil, this air," and a line about parents and their parents. The poem's opening sets out a self that is shared, not owned.

Study question for this tree: what does a shared self look like in a tool that one person owns and runs? The poem answers with the body and the soil. A tree answers with its commits and its readers.

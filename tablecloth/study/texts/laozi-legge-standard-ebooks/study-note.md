## Legge's method, from the translator's preface and endnotes (read 20261018.175845)

Read from `epub/text/translators-preface.xhtml` and `epub/text/endnotes.xhtml`. Paraphrased; no verse is quoted.

- **The series.** Legge's preface says the two volumes fulfil a promise made in an earlier volume: to give translations of the Taoist works, so the System of Taoism can be seen whole. The Tao Te Ching is one of the three works named.
- **The shape.** The volumes add four shorter Taoist treatises, and analyses of several books of Chuang-tzu. The ebook keeps the translator's preface and drops the parts that concern his other works.
- **The romanization.** Legge uses Wade-Giles for Chinese words, and keeps the names Laozi and Tao Te Ching in their familiar forms.

What this gives the study: the translation was made as one part of a larger effort to show a whole tradition, not as a standalone text.

## The book's structure, in reading order (read 20261018.175911)

Read from the spine in `epub/content.opf`. Titles only; no verse is quoted.

1. Preface by James Legge (`translators-preface.xhtml`).
2. Four introductions (`translators-intro-1` to `-4`): whether Taoism is older than Laozi; the texts of the Tao Te Ching and the Chuang-tzu, and their authenticity; the meaning of the name Tao and the chief points of Taoist belief; the accounts of Laozi and Chuang-tzu given by Ssu-ma Ch'ien.
3. The Tao Te Ching itself, in two parts (`tao-te-ching.xhtml`).
4. Endnotes, colophon, and the uncopyright page.

What this gives the study: the book opens with a case for its own history before it gives the text. The reader meets the questions of authenticity first.

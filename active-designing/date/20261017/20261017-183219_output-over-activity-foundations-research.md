## The one idea

Output is what the whole pier produced, not the hour a hand spent. Every elder foundation we reread
points the same direction: the count that matters is what the tree gained, and every act has a
multiplier, positive or negative, that decides whether it was worth doing.

## What the elders give us, by theme

**Measure the whole.** `output-over-activity` says output belongs to the three hands together
(Keaton, counsel, the bench). `the-return-that-feeds-everyone` and `the-water-and-the-work` say the
same of households and water: a system that renews itself keeps books on both sides of what it
spends. We keep the unit; we add the second side as a partner indicator.

**Leverage and its negative twin.** `output-over-activity` names negative leverage: taking back work
a hand can do, arriving unprepared, a gate nobody trusts. Our rota gives the cheapest counter. A
round opened with the cold run already read is a round with positive leverage.

**Count the steps, do not admire them.** `the-three-depths-of-removal` and `galls-law-and-the-grown-system`
both reduce work by counting what survives. Gall says a working complex system grew from a simple one;
the three depths name what a removal costs at each depth. Together they make simplification a
measured act with a target set before the step is examined.

**Stop the line for a fault.** `standfast-the-stopped-line` and `reds-first` (the ledger law)
say a known wrong halts the work until it is repaired and witnessed. Output-over-activity agrees:
a red booked is output saved, because it stops the building over a fault.

**Trust follows proof.** `nothing-to-give-custody-first-principle` puts safety before any measure: build
nothing that destroys. `output-over-activity` says trust rises with what a hand has proven. The two
meet at the custody gate: an automated stop is licensed by the witness that binds it, never by
confidence.

**One clock, one mark.** `the-clock-and-the-mark` gives every piece of work a stamp that says when
and a name that says what. That is the measurement habit output tracking needs: a row is only
comparable to another row if both were stamped by the same clock.

**Connective tissue is output.** `the-fascia-and-the-way-home` says the tree lives or dies by its
links. A link is work that other work stands on. Fascia density (how many living pages a page
connects to, honestly counted) is an output measure with a natural partner: broken links.

**The breach, the molt, and the mitra shed.** `the-breach`, `what-a-molt-is`, and
`the-mitra-shed` say how a large change moves without losing testimony: a new living mutant beside a
kept fossil. For output tracking, this means the old dated project files stay put, and a
development summary is written as a new dated file.

**Sameness and the compression.** `sameness-is-the-macro` values what stays public and explicit.
An indicator is only useful if its definition is public, so every project's measure table names the
unit and the reader who checks it.

**The round is the meeting.** `output-over-activity` calls a round the meeting of this pier. An audit
is a round whose output is the reading itself.

| Speed of a round | correctness of the round's claims |

## Tags and crosslinks

A tag system only earns its place if it is cheap to apply and checkable. We propose one front-matter
line per dated file, `**Kin:**` already in use, and one controlled set of topic tags named in the project
README, not a free-form list. Crosslinks follow the fascia rule: a page links to its nearest parent and
its nearest sibling, and the count of links is the density.

# Gall and Conway -- why we start simple

**Gall's Law:** a complex system that works is invariably found to have evolved from a simple
system that worked. A complex system designed from scratch never works and cannot be patched
into working; you have to start over with a working simple system. So we begin nearly-blank
and grow each piece from one that already stands green.

**Conway's Law:** a system's structure mirrors the communication structure of those who make
it. One maintainer working methodically, reading every line, builds a tree shaped to be read
every line; a fleet of parallel hands builds a fleet of parallel trees. We shape the tree to
match how the work truly happens rather than how an org chart imagines it.

Together they give the strategy a name: **Simple, Lovable, Complete**, in that order. Simple
enough to hold in one mind, lovable enough to want to keep, and only then complete.
